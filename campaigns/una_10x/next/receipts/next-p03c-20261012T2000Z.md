## Reading (H3, direction not a gate)

- **Worker count dominates; numba threads per worker are neutral.** At fixed 4-cpu
  affinity, throughput improves monotonically with worker count (W1T4 36.9 → W2T2
  22.0 → W4T1 15.7 median), and at W4 the thread count does not matter (W4T4 14.9 ≈
  W4T1 15.7, within the 1.7 s rep spread). Per the pre-registered decision rule:
  the "W1T4 wins" branch (numba threads help, workers hurt) is **refuted** — the
  worker/coordinator topology axis is what pays.
- **The pre-merge W4 oversubscription signature does not reproduce as a throughput
  cliff on the current tree.** P01 measured per-row wall stretched at W4 (1.1568,
  `peak_busy_procs=5 > cpuset 4`) and hypothesized coordinator oversubscription as
  the mechanism. Re-measured on the merged tree, W4 is the *best* configuration with
  no W2→W4 reversal — oversubscription is not binding here. Consistent with the
  merged engine halving fold self time (15.6 → 7.8 s; 4.4% of serial, P01R): the
  thread-parallelizable region worth accelerating is small, and whatever coordinator
  stretch existed pre-merge no longer reverses the worker-count scaling.
- **Driver half of H3 (Python-loop vs compiled driver): resolved by the merge** — the
  compiled outer replay is in-tree (P01R); recorded, not re-measured.
- P03C design input (no decision taken here): the live scaling axis is worker count
  (R2 pool-efficiency repair target unchanged); `threads_per_worker` default 1
  remains the right default on 4-cpu windows, and no evidence suggests numba thread
  admission would pay on this cohort.
